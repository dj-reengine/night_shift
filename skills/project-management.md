# Project Management for Public-Sector Engagements (ReEngine Consulting)

## 0. When to use this skill

Use when:
- Writing a Project Management Plan (PMP) or "management approach" section for a government RFP/RFQ.
- Chartering, kicking off, or planning a ReEngine engagement (schedule, WBS, staffing, communication, risk, quality).
- Tracking progress: status reports, in-progress summaries and reports, earned value, buffer status, issue and risk logs.
- Managing scope changes, contract modifications, and deliverable acceptance.
- Choosing a delivery approach (predictive, agile, hybrid, CCPM).
- Closing an engagement: final report acceptance, lessons learned, transition.
- Helping a client run its own improvement implementation as a project.

Related skills: gov-proposal-pmp (PMP section writing), services-qcp (quality control plans), project-status-report, risk-register, raci-matrix, schedule-monte-carlo, scope-creep-response. This file is the methodology foundation; those skills handle specific artifacts.

## 2. Core concepts

### 2.1 Project, program, portfolio, operations

- Project: temporary endeavor creating a unique product, service, or result (a GQI engagement; a kaizen series; a system requirements effort).
- Program: related projects managed together for benefits (a multi-division improvement initiative such as a six-program DEQ engagement).
- Portfolio: collection of projects and programs managed to strategic objectives (an agency's CI portfolio).
- Operations: ongoing work. Improvement projects hand results to operations (control plans, standard work).

### 2.2 PMBOK 7: principles

PMBOK 7 is principles-based. Twelve principles of project delivery:
1. Be a diligent, respectful, and caring steward.
2. Create a collaborative project team environment.
3. Effectively engage with stakeholders.
4. Focus on value.
5. Recognize, evaluate, and respond to system interactions.
6. Demonstrate leadership behaviors.
7. Tailor based on context.
8. Build quality into processes and deliverables.
9. Navigate complexity.
10. Optimize risk responses.
11. Embrace adaptability and resiliency.
12. Enable change to achieve the envisioned future state.

Public-sector translation: stewardship includes public funds and records law; value means public value (QT/OE); stakeholder engagement includes the authorizing environment (legislators, boards, unions, oversight); enabling change links directly to change-management.md.

### 2.3 PMBOK 7: performance domains

| Domain | What good looks like | ReEngine engagement practice |

### 2.5 Triple constraint and beyond

Scope, schedule, cost, with quality, risk, resources, and stakeholder satisfaction. In government consulting, scope and price are usually fixed in the contract (firm fixed price by deliverable, time and materials with a not-to-exceed, or task orders). Flexibility lives in sequencing, approach, and client effort, which makes change control and expectation management essential.

## 3. Development approaches and method selection

### 3.1 Approaches

| Approach | Fit | Government examples |
|---|---|---|
| Predictive (waterfall) | Requirements clear; deliverables defined by contract; compliance-heavy | Final reports, SOP libraries, assessments with fixed deliverables |
| Iterative / incremental | Learn and refine in cycles | Draft and revise SOP sets; dashboard prototypes |
| Agile (Scrum) | High uncertainty; frequent feedback; software or product work | Dashboard or portal development; digital forms |
| Flow (Kanban) | Continuous stream of variable work | Improvement action tracking; service request handling; Journey Boards |
| CCPM | Resource-constrained, multi-project, reliability matters | Engagement portfolios; IT portfolios |
| Hybrid | Mixed deliverables | GQI: predictive phases with agile or flow execution of actions |

### 3.2 Selection guide

| Question | If yes, lean toward |
|---|---|
| Are deliverables and acceptance criteria fixed in the contract? | Predictive backbone |
| Will the client's needs evolve as they see prototypes? | Iterative or agile within the backbone |
| Is the work a stream of small items (recommendations, SOPs)? | Kanban flow with WIP limits |
| Are key client experts (the constraint) shared across many efforts? | CCPM staggering and buffers |
| Is the client's governance strongly gate-based? | Stage gates (PRINCE2-like) |
| Is software built by the client or vendor? | Agile for build, predictive for contract reporting |

ReEngine default: a hybrid. The GQI seven phases form the predictive structure (Charter It, Vision It, Map It, Gap It, Build It, Launch It, Track It), each with deliverables and acceptance; the work inside Build It and Launch It is managed as a flow of actions on a Journey Board with BRAG status; client experts' time is protected with CCPM-style staggering.

### 3.3 GQI phases mapped to PM process groups

| GQI phase | PM focus | Typical deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| Charter It | Initiating; planning | Charter (problem, goal, scope, roles), work plan, communication plan, kickoff |
| Vision It | Planning; stakeholder engagement | Strategic Compass, goal unit, WGLL |
| Map It | Executing | Value Stream SOPs (current state), data baseline, interview synopses |
| Gap It | Executing; analysis | Interference diagram, root causes, constraint analysis |
| Build It | Executing | Future state, recommendations, Strategic Path, implementation plan |
| Launch It | Executing; transition | Training, SOP rollout, pilots, mini-launch sessions |
| Track It | Monitoring and controlling; closing | Dashboards, 30/60/90 report-outs, BRAG, final report, lessons learned |

Roles in practice (from the RAG): the Albany municipal court charter listed David Johnson as project manager and Alfredo Mycue as facilitator [Summary, Albany detail]. ReEngine PMP boilerplate names Alfredo Mycue as QA Officer and David Johnson as project manager in the quality control plan (CORE_METH_PMP-Boilerplate). Verify current roles for each new proposal.

### 4.2 Stakeholder identification

Stakeholder register columns: name or role; organization; interest; influence; current engagement (unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading); desired engagement; key concerns; communication preference; owner.

Power-interest grid: manage closely (high power, high interest), keep satisfied (high power, low interest), keep informed (low power, high interest), monitor (low, low).

Government stakeholders to always check: elected officials and their staff, boards and commissions, oversight (auditor, inspector general), unions and employee associations, civil service or HR, legal counsel, IT, records management, procurement, communications or public information office, partner agencies, vendors of existing systems, advocacy groups, the public and applicants.

### 4.3 Kickoff meeting agenda (90 minutes)

1. Welcome and purpose (sponsor), 10 minutes.
2. Introductions and roles, 10.
3. Charter review: problem, goal, scope, deliverables, 20.
4. Approach and schedule (GQI phases), 15.
5. Communication and reporting plan; decision rights, 10.
6. Data and document requests; access logistics, 10.
7. Risks and concerns from the room, 10.
8. Next steps, 5.
Output: meeting minutes with action items within the contract-specified window (the VA SOW in the BOK required minutes within a set number of days; check each contract).

## 5. Planning

### 5.1 Scope and WBS

Rules:
- 100% rule: the WBS includes all work, and only the work, in the scope.
- Decompose to work packages that can be estimated, assigned, and verified (often 8-80 hours).
- Deliverable-oriented: organize by deliverables (or GQI phases), not by departments.
- WBS dictionary: description, owner, acceptance criteria, effort, dependencies.

Example WBS for a GQI engagement (generic):

```
1. Project management
   1.1 Charter and work plan
   1.2 Status reporting (IPS/IPR, monthly reports)
   1.3 Risk and change management
   1.4 Closeout
2. Charter It / Vision It
   2.1 Leadership alignment session
   2.2 Strategic Compass
3. Map It
   3.1 Document review
   3.2 Staff interviews and synopsis
   3.3 Current-state Value Stream SOPs
   3.4 Baseline data analysis
4. Gap It
   4.1 Interference diagram workshops
   4.2 Root cause and constraint analysis
5. Build It
   5.1 Future-state design sessions (kaizen)
   5.2 Recommendations report (draft, final)
   5.3 Strategic Path and implementation plan
   5.4 System requirements (if in scope)
6. Launch It
   6.1 Training and SOP rollout
   6.2 Pilot support
7. Track It
   7.1 Dashboard
   7.2 30/60/90 report-outs
   7.3 Final report and lessons learned
```

### 5.2 Requirements traceability (for solicitations and system work)

| Req ID | Source (RFP section, statute, stakeholder) | Requirement text (verbatim) | Deliverable / WBS | Acceptance criterion | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For proposals, the compliance matrix is a traceability matrix: every "shall" in the solicitation maps to a response location.

### 5.3 Estimating

- Analogous: based on similar past engagements (use RAG Task-Matrix history only if verified).
- Parametric: units times rate (for example SOPs times hours per SOP; interviews times hours per interview including synopsis).
- Bottom-up: sum of work packages.
- Three-point (PERT): E = (O + 4M + P) / 6; standard deviation = (P - O) / 6.
- ReEngine O-G-P: Optimistic, Goal, Pessimistic times used in WGLL; can feed PERT directly.

Worked PERT example (hypothetical example): current-state mapping for one program: O = 3 days, M = 5, P = 10. E = (3 + 20 + 10) / 6 = 5.5 days; SD = 7/6 = 1.17 days.

Include client effort in estimates: staff hours for interviews and workshops, SME reviews of drafts, sponsor reviews. Client SME availability is frequently the schedule constraint.

### 5.4 Scheduling: critical path method (CPM)

Steps: list activities; define dependencies (finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish, start-to-finish) with leads and lags; estimate durations; forward pass (early start, early finish); backward pass (late start, late finish); float = LS - ES; the critical path has zero float.

Worked example (hypothetical example, durations in days):

| Activity | Duration | Predecessor |
|---|---|---|
| A Kickoff | 1 | - |
| B Document review | 5 | A |
| C Interviews | 8 | A |
| D Current-state maps | 6 | B, C |
| E Data baseline | 7 | B |
| F Gap workshops | 3 | D, E |
| G Draft report | 5 | F |

Forward pass: A 0-1; B 1-6; C 1-9; E 6-13; D starts max(6, 9) = 9, ends 15; F starts max(15, 13) = 15, ends 18; G 18-23. Project duration 23 days.
Backward pass: G 18-23; F 15-18; D LF 15, LS 9; E LF 15, LS 8 (float 2); C LF 9, LS 1 (float 0); B LF min(LS of D = 9, LS of E = 8) = 8, LS 3 (float 2); A 0-1.
Critical path: A-C-D-F-G (23 days). Interviews are on the critical path; protect interview scheduling with the client early.

Schedule compression: crashing (add resources to critical activities at least cost) and fast tracking (overlap activities, adding risk). In government work, a common fast-track is running data baseline in parallel with interviews.

Resource leveling and smoothing: adjust for limited facilitators or client SMEs; may lengthen the critical path. CCPM (theory-of-constraints.md Section 5) addresses resource contention and buffers explicitly.

### 5.5 Cost and budget

Cost baseline: time-phased budget by WBS (S-curve). Management reserve (for unknown unknowns; not in baseline) vs contingency reserve (for identified risks; in baseline). In fixed-price government contracts, the price is fixed; internally, track effort against the estimate to protect margin and quality.

### 5.6 Quality planning

Quality management plan elements: standards (contract requirements, ReEngine methodology standards, accessibility, plain language, records rules), quality metrics (deliverable first-pass acceptance, defects found in review, on-time delivery), roles (QA officer), review steps (peer review, QA officer review, client review), and tools (checklists, style guide).

ReEngine practice (CORE_METH_PMP-Boilerplate): deliverables pass internal review and sign-off under a quality control plan with a named QA officer before submission; internal controls describe how work is checked. See the services-qcp skill for full QCP authoring.

Deliverable quality checklist:

```
[ ] Meets contract requirement text and acceptance criteria (cite section)
[ ] All figures sourced and periods labeled; no unsupported claims
[ ] Hypotheticals labeled; projections labeled as projections
[ ] Consistent with prior deliverables and agreed terminology
[ ] Client names, titles, and organization names verified
[ ] Accessibility (headings, alt text, contrast) if required
[ ] Plain language; executive summary present
[ ] Peer review complete; QA officer sign-off
[ ] Version and date on cover; change log updated
```

### 5.7 Resource planning

RACI per deliverable (see raci-matrix skill). Team charter (working agreements, decision rules, meeting norms). ReEngine boilerplate staffing pattern: two staff per program, a partner and an analyst (CORE_METH_PMP-Boilerplate); adjust per contract.

| Audience | Information | Format | Frequency | Owner | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Progress, decisions needed, risks | Briefing, decision brief | Biweekly / as needed | ReEngine PM | Meeting |
| Client project lead | Detailed status, schedule, actions | In-Progress Summary (IPS) | Weekly | ReEngine PM | Email / meeting |
| Steering group | Phase results, issues | In-Progress Report (IPR) | Monthly or per phase | ReEngine PM and client lead | Meeting with deck |
| Staff participants | What is happening and why; their role | Notices, huddles | Per event | Client lead | Email, huddle |
| Oversight / board | Milestones and results | Report | Per contract | Sponsor | Formal |

In-Progress Summaries and Reports (from the ReEngine PMP boilerplate): ReEngine reports progress through brief In-Progress Summaries and more complete In-Progress Reports, and uses decision briefs when a client decision is needed, so the client always knows status, issues, and upcoming decisions (CORE_METH_PMP-Boilerplate; verify exact cadence per contract).

Decision brief template:

```
Decision needed (one sentence):
Background (3-5 sentences, sourced):
Options (2-4) with pros, cons, cost, risk, and effect on goal:
Recommendation (draft) and rationale:
Decision deadline and consequence of delay:

```

The NYSDOT engagement included a formal decision brief for establishing a Statewide Contract Compliance Manager role [Summary], an example of a decision brief deliverable.

### 5.9 Risk management planning

Risk process: plan, identify, analyze (qualitative, quantitative), plan responses, implement, monitor.

Risk statement format: "Because of [cause], [risk event] may occur, which would lead to [effect on objectives]."

Responses: threats (avoid, transfer, mitigate, accept, escalate); opportunities (exploit, share, enhance, accept, escalate).

Probability-impact matrix (1-5 each; score = P x I; 15+ high, 8-14 medium, under 8 low).

Common risks in government engagements:

| Risk | Typical response |
|---|---|
| Key client SMEs unavailable (constraint) | Schedule early; stagger programs; CCPM buffers; sponsor commitment in charter |
| Data access delayed or poor quality | Early data request; manual sampling plan; data caveats in deliverables |
| Leadership change or election | Brief successors; document decisions; sponsor coalition |
| Reorganization during the project | Scenario planning; keep maps useful for funding decisions (Minnesota childcare lesson [Summary]) |
| Union or civil service concerns | Early engagement with labor relations; notice periods; no headcount framing |
| IT system change in flight | "Clean before automating"; requirements deliverable; coordinate with vendor |
| Scope creep from enthusiastic stakeholders | Change control; parking lot; future phase list |
| Procurement or invoicing delays | Clear deliverable acceptance; invoice per contract |
| Staff saturated (no white space) | Hybrid onsite and remote model (Columbus Traffic lesson [Summary]); short sessions |
| Public records exposure of drafts | Label drafts; follow client records guidance |

Quantitative tools: expected monetary value (EMV = probability x impact), decision trees, Monte Carlo schedule simulation (see schedule-monte-carlo skill).

### 5.10 Procurement (when ReEngine subcontracts or partners)

Subcontractor management: scope in writing, flow-down clauses, insurance, small business or DBE/MBE/WBE reporting where applicable, payment terms, deliverable review before client submission. ReEngine has partnered with other firms (for example an engineering consultant on the Columbus traffic study work, and as a subcontractor to a prime on the Virginia Employment Commission work [Summary]); define roles and branding clearly in such teaming.

## 6. Executing

## 7. Monitoring and controlling

### 7.1 Earned value management (EVM)

Definitions:
- PV (planned value): budgeted cost of work scheduled.
- EV (earned value): budgeted cost of work performed.
- AC (actual cost): actual cost of work performed.
- BAC: budget at completion.

Formulas:
- Cost variance CV = EV - AC. Schedule variance SV = EV - PV.
- CPI = EV / AC. SPI = EV / PV.
- EAC (if current CPI continues) = BAC / CPI.
- EAC (atypical variance; future at plan) = AC + (BAC - EV).
- EAC (CPI and SPI both influence) = AC + (BAC - EV) / (CPI x SPI).
- ETC = EAC - AC. VAC = BAC - EAC.
- TCPI (to meet BAC) = (BAC - EV) / (BAC - AC).
- Earned schedule (ES) refinements are useful late in projects when SPI converges to 1.

Worked example (hypothetical example): BAC $200,000. At month 4, PV = $90,000; EV = $75,000; AC = $85,000.
- CV = 75,000 - 85,000 = -$10,000 (over cost). SV = 75,000 - 90,000 = -$15,000 (behind).
- CPI = 75 / 85 = 0.882. SPI = 75 / 90 = 0.833.
- EAC = 200,000 / 0.882 = $226,700. VAC = -$26,700.
- TCPI = (200,000 - 75,000) / (200,000 - 85,000) = 125 / 115 = 1.087: remaining work must be 8.7% more efficient than planned to finish on budget.
Interpretation for a fixed-price contract: the client price does not change; the overrun is ReEngine's margin risk; the schedule slip is the client-visible issue to address in the next IPS.

Simplified EV for deliverable-based contracts: use 0/100 (credit when accepted) or 50/50 (half at start, half at acceptance) rules per work package; report percent of deliverables accepted vs planned.

### 7.2 Schedule control

Track baseline vs forecast milestones; float consumption on the critical path; for CCPM, fever chart. Report early and specifically: "Interviews 60% complete vs 80% planned because two program managers rescheduled; recovery plan: add evening virtual slots; milestone risk: Map It review moves 1 week unless rescheduled by [date]."

### 7.3 Integrated change control

Change request form:

```
CR no.:          Date:          Requested by:
Description of change:
Reason / source (stakeholder request, discovered need, external event):
Impact: scope / schedule / cost (effort and price) / quality / risk / other deliverables
Options (do nothing; accept within scope; contract modification; defer to future phase):
Recommendation (draft):
Decision: approved / rejected / deferred      By:        Date:
Contract modification required? Y/N  Reference:
```

Rules for government contracts: only the contracting officer (or designated authority) can change contract scope or price; project-level agreements with program staff do not modify contracts. Keep a change log; confirm decisions in writing. See scope-creep-response skill for handling requests diplomatically.

### 7.4 Scope validation and deliverable acceptance

1. Submit the deliverable with a transmittal referencing the contract requirement and acceptance criteria.
2. Client review period per contract (commonly 5-15 business days).
3. Consolidated comments (ask the client lead to consolidate).
4. Revise; provide a comment-response matrix.
5. Formal acceptance in writing (signature, email, or acceptance form).
6. Invoice per contract terms.

Comment-response matrix:

| # | Page/section | Reviewer | Comment | Response | Change made (Y/N) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

ReEngine boilerplate emphasizes review and sign-off of deliverables (CORE_METH_PMP-Boilerplate).

### 7.5 Status reporting

Status report outline (weekly or biweekly IPS):

```
Period:                     Overall status (G/Y/R) with reason:
Accomplishments this period:
Planned next period:
Milestones: baseline vs forecast
Deliverables: submitted / in review / accepted
Risks and issues (top 3) with owner and action:
Decisions needed (with date):
Client actions outstanding:
Budget / effort (internal) status:
```

Status color definitions must be explicit: Green on track; Yellow at risk, recoverable within the team; Red will miss a commitment without sponsor action. Do not "watermelon" (green outside, red inside).

### 7.6 Issue management

Issue log: ID, description, date raised, owner, priority, action, due date, status, resolution. Escalation path per governance (client lead, then sponsor, then contracting officer for contractual issues).

## 8. Closing

Closeout checklist:

```
[ ] All deliverables accepted in writing (list with dates)
[ ] Final invoice submitted; payment tracked
[ ] Transition: control plans, SOPs, dashboards, and implementation plan handed to process owners
[ ] Training materials and source files delivered in the agreed formats
[ ] Client data returned or destroyed per contract; access removed
[ ] Lessons learned session (client and internal)
[ ] Past performance summary drafted for the RAG (internal; client approval needed before external use)
[ ] Reference permission requested (if appropriate)
[ ] Follow-up (30/60/90) scheduled if in scope
[ ] Records archived per ReEngine retention
```

Lessons learned format: what happened; impact; root cause; what to repeat; what to change; who should know. Feed them to the RAG 01_PastProjects and 05_ProjectSummaries only through David (agents do not write to the RAG).

Transition-out: the VA SOW (BOK) required a transition-out plan; many government contracts do. Include knowledge transfer sessions, an inventory of artifacts, and contacts.

## 9. Agile, flow, and other frameworks

### 9.1 Agile Practice Guide essentials

Agile values (Agile Manifesto): individuals and interactions, working results, customer collaboration, responding to change. Principles include frequent delivery, sustainable pace, simplicity (maximizing work not done), self-organizing teams, and regular reflection. The PMI Agile Practice Guide describes a continuum of life cycles (predictive, iterative, incremental, agile) and hybrid combinations; tailoring is expected.

### 9.2 Scrum

- Roles (accountabilities): Product Owner (value and backlog), Scrum Master (process and impediments), Developers (doing the work).
- Events: Sprint (1-4 weeks), Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum (15 minutes), Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective.
- Artifacts: Product Backlog (with Product Goal), Sprint Backlog (with Sprint Goal), Increment (with Definition of Done).
- Government fit: digital service delivery, dashboards, portals. Challenges: procurement requiring fixed scope; product owner availability; approval processes. Mitigation: fixed budget and time with prioritized scope; outcome-based deliverables; a client-side product owner with real authority.

User story format: "As a [role], I want [capability] so that [benefit]." Acceptance criteria in Given-When-Then form. Estimation in story points; velocity for forecasting.

### 9.3 Kanban

Practices: visualize the work; limit WIP; manage flow; make policies explicit; implement feedback loops; improve collaboratively. Metrics: cycle time, throughput, WIP, cumulative flow diagrams, aging WIP. ReEngine's Journey Board is a Kanban-style board with weekly targets by value-adding phase (lean.md Section 11). MDOT TPU used a Journey Board and cumulative flow [Summary]; Minnesota Lottery used a Teams Journey Board for procurement buckets [Summary]; SD LRC used a Teams Journey Board [Summary].

### 9.4 PRINCE2

Seven principles: continued business justification; learn from experience; defined roles and responsibilities; manage by stages; manage by exception; focus on products; tailor to suit the project.
Seven themes (practices in the 7th edition): business case, organization, quality, plans, risk, change, progress (the 7th edition adds an explicit focus on people).
Seven processes: starting up, directing, initiating, controlling a stage, managing product delivery, managing stage boundaries, closing.
Key roles: project board (executive, senior user, senior supplier), project manager, team manager, project assurance, project support.
Useful for government: management by exception (tolerances on time, cost, scope, quality, risk, benefit), product descriptions with quality criteria (similar to deliverable acceptance), and stage gates aligned with budget or approval cycles.

### 9.5 Disciplined Agile (PMI)

A toolkit for choosing your way of working (WoW) based on context, with life cycles (agile, lean, continuous delivery, exploratory, program) and process goals. Principles include "context counts," "choice is good," "optimize flow," and "enterprise awareness." Useful when a client's IT organization mixes approaches.

### 9.6 Critical Chain Project Management

See theory-of-constraints.md Section 5. Key PM implications: remove task-level padding and milestones; protect the critical chain with project and feeding buffers; report fever charts; stagger multi-project starts by the scarcest resource; discourage multitasking.

## 10. Government contract project management

### 10.1 Contract types and implications

| Type | Implication for PM |
|---|---|
| Firm fixed price (FFP), often by deliverable | Scope control critical; invoice upon acceptance; margin risk on effort overrun |
| Time and materials (T&M) / labor hour with NTE | Track hours by labor category; report burn rate; approval for NTE increase |
| Task order / IDIQ / master agreement | Each task order has its own scope, price, and deliverables; manage as separate projects in a program |
| Cost reimbursable | Rare for ReEngine; detailed cost accounting |
| Subcontract to a prime | Flow-down terms; report to the prime; client contact rules |

CLINs and line items: some contracts (especially federal) price by contract line item numbers; track deliverables and invoices by CLIN. State and local contracts often price by task or deliverable.

### 10.2 What a PMP section in a proposal usually must show

Compiled from common solicitation requirements (and the sample PMPs on Drive, located but not read in full):
1. Project organization and key personnel (roles, responsibilities, qualifications, time commitment, organizational chart).
2. Approach to managing scope, schedule, cost, quality, risk, communication, and change.
3. Work plan and schedule (Gantt or milestone table) tied to the solicitation tasks.
4. Status reporting (frequency, format, meetings).
5. Deliverable review and acceptance process.
6. Quality control and assurance (QC plan, QA officer).
7. Risk management (initial risk register).
8. Issue escalation.
9. Subcontractor management (if applicable).
10. Transition and knowledge transfer.
See the gov-proposal-pmp skill for section authoring.

### 10.3 ReEngine PMP boilerplate elements (CORE_METH_PMP-Boilerplate, WY DEQ-based)

- Staffing: two staff per program (a partner and an analyst); verify per solicitation.
- Progress reporting: In-Progress Summaries (brief, frequent) and In-Progress Reports (fuller, periodic).
- Decision briefs when client decisions are required.
- Review and sign-off on deliverables.
- Internal controls describing how ReEngine checks its own work.
- Quality control plan naming Alfredo Mycue as QA Officer and David Johnson as project manager.
Adapt wording to each solicitation; do not copy client-specific references from the source engagement.

### 10.4 Compliance and administration checklist

### 10.5 Working within public-sector governance

- Meetings may be subject to open meetings law (boards, councils); plan for public materials.
- Legislative calendars and budget cycles drive timing (deliver recommendations before budget requests or sessions where they matter; Minnesota childcare mapping supported legislative funding decisions [Summary]).
- Union notification or meet-and-confer may be required before changes to working conditions.
- Elections and leadership turnover: document decisions and rationale; brief transitions.
- Procurement rules restrict what consultants can do in subsequent procurements (organizational conflict of interest when writing system requirements).

## 13. Integration with the other four methodologies

- Lean: kaizen events are mini-projects (charter, plan, logistics, follow-up). Use Lean (visual boards, standard work, waste removal) on the project process itself: standard templates for IPS, consistent deliverable formats, Full-Kit before workshops (data, attendees, room).
- Six Sigma: DMAIC tollgates align to phase gates; quality metrics for deliverables; SPC is rarely needed for projects but useful in Track It.
- TOC: CCPM for schedules; identify the project's constraint (often client SME time or sponsor decisions) and subordinate the plan to it; avoid multitasking across engagements.
- Change management: PMBOK 7 principle "enable change"; integrate the change management plan (sponsor roadmap, communications, training, resistance management) into the PM plan; the PM owns technical delivery, the change lead owns people-side delivery, and they share a single schedule.

## 14. Decision guides

### 14.1 Status color

Green: all milestones forecast on baseline; no high risks without response. Yellow: a milestone forecast late but recoverable within tolerance, or a high risk with response in progress. Red: a contractual milestone will be missed or a deliverable will not meet acceptance criteria without sponsor or contract action.

### 14.2 Is this a change request?

| Request | Change? |
|---|---|
| Clarifies how to do in-scope work | No; document |
| Adds a deliverable, program, site, or significant effort | Yes; CR and possibly contract mod |
| Changes the acceptance criteria | Yes |
| Shifts dates due to client delay | Yes (schedule); document cause |
| Asks for "just one more" SOP or workshop | Evaluate effort; often yes |

### 14.3 Escalate or handle?

Handle at team level if within tolerances. Escalate to sponsor if a tolerance will be breached, a decision is outside the client lead's authority, or stakeholder conflict blocks progress. Escalate to contracting officer for scope, price, or contract term changes.

### 14.4 Method selection

See Section 3.2.

## 15. Facilitation scripts and questions

Kickoff: "Our job is to help you reach [goal] by [date]. To get there, we need three things from you: time from the people who know the work, access to data, and quick decisions when we bring options. Here is exactly when we will need each."

Scope conversation: "That's a valuable idea. It's outside our current scope, so let's put it on the future-phase list. If it becomes a priority now, we'll bring you a change request that shows the impact on schedule and cost so you can decide."

Risk workshop: "Imagine it's the end of the project and it went badly. What happened?" (pre-mortem)

Status meeting: "Here's where we are against plan, the one thing at risk, and the decision we need from you today."

Late client reviews: "To hold the [date] milestone, we need consolidated comments by [date]. If that's difficult, we can (a) narrow the review to sections X and Y, or (b) move the milestone by [n] days. Which works better?"

Lessons learned: "What should we keep doing? What should we stop? What should we start? What surprised us?"

Questions bank:
- Who can say yes to this deliverable?
- What would make you reject it?
- What external date (board meeting, legislative session, budget submission) matters?
- Who will feel this change most, and have they heard about it from their supervisor?
- What else is competing for your staff's time during these months?

## 16. Common pitfalls

1. Vague acceptance criteria leading to endless revision cycles.
2. Treating program-level agreements as contract changes.
3. Underestimating client effort and SME availability.
4. Status reports that hide problems ("watermelon").
5. No single consolidated client comment set.
6. Ignoring public records and open meetings implications.
7. Over-planning detail early; under-planning stakeholder engagement.
8. Multitasking across engagements; key staff stretched (TOC).
9. Scope creep from workshops ("while you're here").
10. Neglecting transition and sustainment; results fade after closeout.
11. Copying boilerplate with another client's name or specifics.
12. Claiming outcomes in closeout that are targets.

## 19. Glossary

- AC, EV, PV, BAC, EAC, ETC, VAC: earned value terms (Section 7.1).
- Baseline: approved version of scope, schedule, or cost against which performance is measured.
- CLIN: contract line item number.
- Change control board: body that approves or rejects changes.
- CPI, SPI: cost and schedule performance indices.
- Critical path: longest path through the network; zero float.
- Decision brief: short options-and-recommendation document for a decision.
- Definition of Done: Scrum quality standard for an increment.
- Float (slack): time an activity can slip without delaying the project.
- IPS, IPR: ReEngine In-Progress Summary and In-Progress Report.
- Management by exception: PRINCE2 principle using tolerances.
- NTE: not-to-exceed.
- PERT: three-point estimate (O + 4M + P) / 6.
- PMP: Project Management Plan (document) or Project Management Professional (credential).
- RACI: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
- Requirements traceability matrix: links requirements to deliverables and tests.
- TCPI: to-complete performance index.
- WBS: work breakdown structure.

## Appendix C. Client-run implementation as a project

After ReEngine delivers recommendations, the client often runs implementation itself. Give the client a light PM kit:
1. Implementation charter per recommendation group (owner, goal, scope, dates).
2. A single action board (Journey Board or Kanban) with BRAG status and WIP limits per owner.
3. A monthly steering review of BRAG, metrics, and decisions needed.
4. A change log for policy and SOP changes (version control; who approved).
5. A benefits tracker: baseline, current, target, source.
6. A 30/60/90-day report-out schedule (EPA Lean Government guide practice).
The NH VR focus-group BRAG with 46 observations (19.6% Red; 26.1% Blue or Green [Summary]) is the kind of snapshot that shows leaders where implementation is stuck. Do not rush items to Blue; require evidence of completion and one full cycle for training-type actions (CORE_METH_BRAG-Tool-Logic).
